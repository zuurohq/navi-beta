An automated strategy for Micro Nasdaq futures, built for one job: passing prop-firm evaluations without blowing the daily loss limit.

What it is

A TradingView strategy that trades MNQ on the 1-minute chart during the New York morning. It decides when to enter, where the stop goes, where the target goes, and when to get out. It sends each order to your trade copier as a message, so your broker account follows automatically.

It has a prop-firm risk module built in: it tracks the day's profit and loss including the open trade, and refuses a setup when the risk would not fit inside the room left under your daily limit. That is the part most strategies do not have, and it is why this one exists.

Straight talk

If you only read one section, read this one. These are the things that will surprise you if nobody tells you now.

Most trades lose

The win rate is 35%. Roughly two out of every three trades are losers. It makes money because the average winner is $923 and the average loser is $400 — winners are about 2.3 times bigger. If a run of six losses in a row would make you turn it off, this is not for you.

Almost half of all months are down

Across 37 months, 20 were positive and 17 were not. That is 54%. A losing month is normal, not a sign that something broke.

The worst drawdown was large

The biggest peak-to-trough fall was $14,513. On a $50,000 account at evaluation sizing that is about 29% of the account. Most prop-firm accounts would not survive it. That is exactly why the default size is for an evaluation, and why funded accounts should be traded much smaller.

Two things the backtest does not tell you.
1. The tester fills orders at the close of the bar that triggered them. Filling on the next bar instead — closer to live — gives about 2.7% less profit over the full history. Treat every figure above as the optimistic end.
2. Stops are judged on 1-minute closes, so a fast move can carry you past the stop before you are out. A real example from live use: stop at 29711.00, filled at 29701.75 — 37 ticks beyond it, against a modelled 2 ticks. Size for that, not for the ideal.
