NAVI Beta

An automated strategy for Micro Nasdaq futures with a daily loss limit built into the engine — not bolted on afterwards. Across 631 trading days of backtesting at its default size, no day closed beyond that limit.

The daily limit is arithmetic, not hope

Before every trade the engine checks the stop-out cost against what is left of the day's limit and refuses the setup if it does not fit. The room shrinks by what the day has already lost, so a day cannot arithmetically lose more than about $966.

What this does not say.

Costs. It holds at modelled costs, and the margin is $3.80 — less than one bad fill. How bad a real fill gets, nobody has measured, including us. What we can say is how much it would matter: re-running the whole backtest at worse fills puts the profit factor at 1.06 at 8 ticks per side and 0.96 — a loss — at 12. Roughly two thirds of trades exit at the stop, so fill quality is the whole margin here.

Closed days. It says where days finished, not that the account never dipped below the limit with a trade open.

Whose limit. The $1,000 is the strategy's own, not your firm's, and it scales with size — $250 at 2 micros. Check your firm's rules yourself.

How it does against your firm

A pass rate is not one number. It depends on the firm, the plan and the account size — a $50,000 evaluation at one firm is a different problem from the same size at another. So rather than quote the flattering one, here is the whole grid: 63 evaluation plans and 57 payout plans across 14 firms, each replayed trade by trade against that firm's own rules.
